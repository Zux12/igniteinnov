Ignite AI™ Ethical Foundation

Maqasid al-Shariah and Responsible AI

Ignite AI™ uses a Maqasid al-Shariah-informed ethical lens to support the responsible design, deployment and governance of artificial intelligence. The framework helps us ask not only whether an AI system can perform a task, but whether its use protects people, promotes benefit, prevents harm and preserves human accountability.

An ethical foundation, not a religious ruling

Ignite AI™ does not independently interpret Shariah, issue fatwas or claim religious authority. Maqasid al-Shariah is used as an ethical governance reference, while formal religious determinations remain with qualified scholars and recognised authorities.

What is Maqasid al-Shariah?

Maqasid al-Shariah refers to the higher objectives and intended benefits of Islamic law. It provides a structured way to consider public benefit, justice, dignity, prevention of harm and responsible human conduct when evaluating new circumstances and technologies.

Ignite AI™ governance position: Maqasid al-Shariah complements—not replaces—responsible AI standards, applicable laws, professional obligations, technical risk management and qualified human review.

Protection of Faith

Hifz al-Din

Respect religious integrity, avoid fabricated religious claims and preserve the role of qualified scholars.

❤️

Protection of Life

Hifz al-Nafs

Prioritise safety, human wellbeing and safeguards against foreseeable physical or psychological harm.

🧠

Protection of Intellect

Hifz al-‘Aql

Promote verification, explainability, truthful information and protection from manipulation or misinformation.

👨‍👩‍👧

Protection of Lineage and Dignity

Hifz al-Nasl

Respect family, identity, privacy, honour and the responsible handling of personal information.

💼

Protection of Wealth

Hifz al-Mal

Protect intellectual property, confidential assets and users from fraud, exploitation and unjust loss.

How Ignite AI™ Applies the Five Objectives

The following mapping translates the ethical objectives into practical AI governance expectations. It is an internal governance interpretation and remains subject to technical, legal, ethical and future Shariah advisory review.

Maqasid objective AI governance requirement Ignite AI™ implementation direction
Hifz al-Din Religious integrity and clear boundaries of authority. No fatwas, no claim of Shariah authority, no fabricated religious sources, and referral of religious determinations to qualified scholars.
Hifz al-Nafs Safety, proportionality and prevention of harm. Human oversight, risk-based controls, no autonomous high-impact decisions and escalation where potentially harmful outcomes are identified.
Hifz al-‘Aql Truthfulness, verification and protection from manipulation. Criteria-based assessment, uncertainty disclosure, hallucination controls, traceable outputs and clear separation between evidence and inference.
Hifz al-Nasl Dignity, privacy, honour and responsible data use. Data minimisation, restricted access, protection of confidential submissions and avoidance of irrelevant personal characteristics in scoring.
Hifz al-Mal Protection of wealth, rights and valuable information. Safeguards for intellectual property, unpublished research, organisational data and prevention of fraudulent or exploitative AI use.

From Ethical Purpose to Operational Control

Ignite AI™ organises its Islamic ethical approach into three connected governance layers.

1

Ethical Foundation

Maqasid al-Shariah explains why AI should protect human interests, prevent harm and promote responsible benefit.

2

Governance Principles

Amanah, ‘Adl, Tabayyun, Maslahah, privacy, transparency, accountability and meaningful human oversight guide conduct.

3

Operational Controls

Access controls, data minimisation, documented criteria, assessment IDs, review mechanisms and escalation procedures turn principles into practice.
